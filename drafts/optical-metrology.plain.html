What Is Optical Metrology?

Optical metrology uses light to measure what touch cannot: dimension, surface form, temperature, alignment, and composition, often at sub-micron precision and without contacting the part. Interferometry, spectroscopy, and optical triangulation are the workhorse techniques behind semiconductor inspection, precision manufacturing, and scientific instrumentation, and each one lives or dies on the quality of the optics that shape, filter, and relay the measurement beam.

How Optical Metrology Works

1. Illumination

A source — laser, LED, or broadband — is shaped and collimated by precision optics before it reaches the part or sample under test.

2. Interaction

Light reflects, refracts, or scatters off the target. The way it changes encodes the dimension, surface, or property being measured.

3. Detection

Imaging and relay optics deliver the modified light to a detector, where it's converted into a measurement with the precision the application demands.

Core Measurement Techniques

Interferometry

Splits and recombines coherent light to measure displacement, surface figure, and vibration to fractions of a wavelength.

Spectroscopy

Resolves light by wavelength to identify material composition, coating thickness, and thermal or chemical state.

Optical Triangulation

Projects a beam or pattern onto a surface and calculates position from the geometry of its reflection, for fast non-contact profiling.
